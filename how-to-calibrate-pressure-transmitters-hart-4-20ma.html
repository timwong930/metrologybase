How do you calibrate a 4–20 mA or HART pressure transmitter?

Apply a known pressure, measure the transmitter's actual electrical output, and compare it to the expected signal. For HART devices, separately verify the digital process value and only perform trims when justified. The Additel ADT673 combines pressure measurement with mA/voltage measurement, 24 V transmitter supply and an optional HART communicator.

Use the transmitter's own instructions and your organization's calibration procedure for test sequence, stabilization, tolerances, traceability and uncertainty. The five-point profile below is illustrative, not a universal requirement.

1. Build the right test setup

You will need

  • A traceably calibrated ADT673 with pressure range and uncertainty suitable for the transmitter.
  • An external manual or controlled pressure source, rated tubing, appropriate media, fittings and isolation hardware.
  • Electrical test leads and, for a two-wire transmitter, an appropriate 24 V loop supply or correctly configured external supply.
  • The transmitter datasheet, LRV/URV, transfer function, tolerance and safe isolation procedure.

Know what the ADT673 can—and cannot—do

The built-in pressure sensor is the reference measurement; an external source makes the pressure. Its electrical section measures DC mA or V and can provide 24 V loop power. It does not function as a precision voltage/current source for calibrating an electrical receiver.

The HART communicator is an ordering option. A non-HART ADT673 can still read analog output from a transmitter that happens to support HART.

Before connection: Follow lockout/isolation, venting and electrical safety requirements. Never exceed the maximum allowable pressure of the lowest-rated component. Select a compatible clean medium; verify supply polarity and circuit limits before energizing. An ammeter connects in series with the loop, not across a powered 24 V supply.

2. Calibrate a conventional 4–20 mA pressure transmitter

This procedure checks the complete pressure-to-analog-output chain. It is appropriate for a two-wire loop-powered pressure transmitter when wired and powered according to the specific device's instructions.

  1. Confirm the transmitter's range.

    Identify its lower and upper range values (LRV/URV), pressure type (gauge/absolute/differential), linear or square-root output, supply requirements and maximum allowable pressure. Example below: 0–100 psig, linear, 4–20 mA.

  2. Isolate the instrument and assemble the pressure path.

    Connect the pressure source, ADT673 reference and transmitter to an appropriately rated common manifold. Leak-check; vent and zero only as permitted by the pressure sensor's instructions and the test method.

  3. Connect the electrical section.

    On the ADT673 select the electrical current measurement channel. If using ADT673 24 V loop power, follow the manufacturer's terminal diagram and verify the transmitter is compatible with the 24 V ±1 V, 30 mA maximum supply. If the loop already has an external supply, do not also enable internal loop power.

  4. Save the as-found results before making adjustments.

    Use Tasks → Pressure Transmitter on the ADT673, choose the analog mA output, pressure input scale and transfer function. The manual §4.2 documents this task setup. Capture the initial response before zeroing or trimming the transmitter.

  5. Apply known pressures.

    For this example test at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 psig; allow pressure and signal to stabilize at each step. Record reference pressure, actual mA, expected mA and deviation. Repeat in the descending direction when the required procedure includes hysteresis.

  6. Judge against the correct tolerance.

    Use the manufacturer's required accuracy basis and your uncertainty/decision rule. Determine whether you are judging analog output, digital PV or the entire measurement chain; these are different measurands.

  7. Adjust only when authorized, then repeat.

    Document as-found data first. If correction is needed, follow the transmitter manufacturer procedure for zero/span or trim, then run the points again and save as-left data. Depressurize and restore connections after testing.

Source: Additel user manual §4.2, PDF pp. 53–55 ↗. Exact menu labels may vary with firmware.

3. Example: 0–100 psi to 4–20 mA

For a linear transmitter, expected output is scaled between its output endpoints:

Expected mA = 4 + 16 × (P − LRV) / (URV − LRV)Here P is applied/reference pressure in the same units as LRV and URV. This equation does not describe square-root or custom transfers.

Worked calculation: At 50 psi, 12.032 − 12.000 = +0.032 mA. Relative to a 16 mA output span, that equals +0.20% of span, corresponding to +0.20 psi of input-equivalent error on a 100 psi range. These readings are fictional teaching values, not ADT673 or transmitter test data.

The ADT673's published DC-current measurement accuracy is ±(0.01% of reading + 1.5 µA) between 8 °C and 38 °C. At 12 mA, the corresponding measurement specification is about ±2.7 µA (±0.0027 mA), before combining pressure-reference uncertainty or other terms. Do not mistake instrument resolution or the electrical specification alone for the total calibration uncertainty. Source: ADT673 electrical table ↗.

4. What about voltage-output transmitters?

Set the ADT673 electrical channel to DC voltage measure rather than current measure, then connect to the transmitter's designated voltage output and common/return terminals, following both manuals. Check that the applied voltage remains within the ADT673's ±30 V measurement range. Do not put the mA input across a voltage source.

Example: 1–5 V output

For a linear 0–100 psi transmitter, the five expected outputs are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 V. More generally: V = Vlow + (Vhigh − Vlow) × (P − LRV) / (URV − LRV).

Power is device-dependent

A three-wire or four-wire voltage transmitter may require an external supply, separate excitation wiring or a supply voltage different from 24 V. Confirm requirements before connecting the ADT673's loop-power terminals. The ADT673 is measuring the output, not simulating the sensor input.

Other transmitter types: Its electrical section can read an appropriate mA or voltage output from a non-pressure transmitter, but the ADT673's built-in pressure sensor is not a reference for temperature, level, flow or humidity. Those calibrations require a suitable independent stimulus and traceable reference.

5. Calibrate a HART pressure transmitter

HART places digital data on a compatible analog loop. It does not mean the digital PV, commanded analog output (AO), and independently measured physical mA are automatically equal. Check the instrument's sensor and its D/A output separately.

Manufacturer safety condition: Additel says the ADT673 operates as the HART master and instructs that the HART device be separated from the control system before connecting. Use the transmitter manual, local work authorization and Additel's factory drawings for electrical hookups; never casually bridge a live DCS/SIS loop or apply two loop supplies.

  1. Verify HART is fitted and wire one approved configuration.

    Use the optional HART-enabled ADT673. Select the corresponding internal/external supply and internal/external load setting. Check loop polarity, 24 V compatibility, communications resistance and wiring against the linked figures.

  2. Open the HART electrical channel and search.

    Under electrical measurement, select HART. The ADT673 begins at poll address 0 and can scan addresses 1–15 if needed (per the published manual). Connect to the intended device; confirm identity/tag, units and configured range before proceeding.

  3. Read the digital PV against the pressure reference.

    Apply known pressure points; compare the ADT673 reference pressure to the transmitter's HART process variable (PV). Record error at each point. The user manual lets the operator choose the HART value used in a transmitter task.

  4. Independently check analog output.

    Read the instrument's AO data and compare to a verified physical loop-current reading using the permitted wiring arrangement. The HART current-loop test can command values in the 4–20 mA range for devices supporting that service; do not interpret commanded current alone as a measured analog output.

  5. Decide whether a configuration change or trim is needed.

    Reranging changes LRV/URV. Sensor trim changes the mapping between actual pressure and digital PV. D/A current trim adjusts physical analog current to agree with the transmitter's intended output. They solve different problems. Some transmitters do not support some services; always follow that transmitter's approved manual.

  6. Save as-found / as-left records and restore service.

    Document the original tag, configured range, pressure points, digital PV, physical current, uncertainties, changes performed, and repeated as-left results. Return the transmitter to its approved operating configuration and verify restoration with the process owner.

Manufacturer references: manual §2.5 (HART connection, PV, service and trim), §4.2 (transmitter tasks) and chapter 8 (full communicator with DD files).

Which ADT673 configuration is right?

Compare the exact pressure module, accuracy code, working medium, port and HART option before purchasing. A model advertised as reaching 60,000 psi does not necessarily offer the highest available accuracy over that range.
