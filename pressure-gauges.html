Pressure gauge buying guide

Choose the gauge
for the job.

A vendor-neutral comparison of 21 current digital test gauges, reference gauges, hazardous-area gauges, differential gauges, panel replacements, and mechanical process gauges. Specifications are summarized from manufacturer documentation; always verify the exact ordered configuration.

Most important rule
Accuracy numbers are not interchangeable.

0.05% FS, 0.05% of reading, and a 0.05% total-error-band specification can behave very differently. MetrologyBase keeps the manufacturer's stated basis visible instead of ranking by the smallest number.

01Pick the pressure type

Gauge, absolute, compound, vacuum, or differential.

02Use the right range

A needlessly large FS range can destroy useful accuracy at low pressure.

03Check total performance

Temperature, stability, media, overpressure, and uncertainty matter.

04Then compare workflow

Logging, Bluetooth, safety approvals, fittings, and software.

Quick shortlist

Start here by use case.

Highest precisionAdditel 685 / KELLER LEX1 / WIKA CPG1500

Start with the actual range and uncertainty requirement; all three offer precision-focused configurations.

Rugged field referenceCrystal XP3i / Fluke 700G

Strong choices where portability, field durability, and established pressure-test workflows matter.

Hazardous areasXP3i, 685Ex, 681AEx, 601Ex, DPI 104-IS

Certification details differ. Match the exact gas group, zone/division, and temperature class to the site.

Lower-cost digital replacementAdditel 680A / WIKA CPG1200 / Ashcroft DG25 / KELLER LEO2

Better fit for indication and service work than precision-reference calibration.

Differential pressureAdditel 685 DP / Crystal XP2i-DP

Choose based on media compatibility, static-pressure limits, range, and required accuracy.

Permanent process gaugeWIKA 232.30 / Ashcroft 1279

Mechanical gauges remain sensible where simplicity, local indication, and no batteries are the priority.

Before you buy

Read the accuracy basis.

%FS / %span: error is tied to the instrument's range. On a 1,000 psi gauge specified at 0.05% FS, the published term is 0.5 psi across the range.

% of reading: the absolute error typically shrinks as the reading falls, though manufacturers may add a floor/range term.

TEB / total error band: can be especially useful because it may include temperature, linearity, hysteresis, and repeatability—but only use the exact definition provided by that manufacturer.

Calibration decisions: do not choose a reference solely from its headline accuracy. Compare your DUT tolerance to the reference's applicable uncertainty/performance across the actual points you will calibrate.
