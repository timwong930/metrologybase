What is metrology?

Metrology is the science of measurement. In practical calibration work, it is the system of references, methods, specifications, uncertainty, and documentation used to decide how much confidence to place in a measurement.

Practical mental modelCalibration is not just making two numbers match. It is understanding the relationship between the device under test, the reference, the method, and the uncertainty of the result.

Calibration vs. verification

Calibration compares an instrument or system against a reference and documents the relationship. Verification checks whether something meets defined requirements. Adjustment changes the instrument to improve its performance.

Those actions are related, but they are not interchangeable. A calibration can be performed without adjustment, and a verification decision depends on the acceptance rules being used.

Accuracy vs. precision

Accuracy describes closeness to an accepted reference value. Precision describes how closely repeated results agree with each other. A system can be repeatable without being close to the reference, which is why the distinction matters.

Traceability

Measurement traceability connects a result through a documented chain of calibrations to recognized references, with uncertainty considered at each step. Traceability belongs to a measurement result and its documented chain—not simply to an instrument by itself.

Uncertainty

Measurement uncertainty expresses quantified doubt around a result. It accounts for meaningful contributors in the measurement process rather than pretending the displayed value is exact.

Later MetrologyBase content will break uncertainty into practical contributors, budgets, coverage factors, and how uncertainty affects TUR and acceptance decisions.
