Pressure · 60,000 PSI

What pressure fittings do I need for 60,000 psi?

At 60,000 psi, ordinary process fittings are no longer the right starting point. Build the system around a purpose-designed ultra-high-pressure cone-and-thread connection family, then keep that connection through the entire 60k pressure path.

SHORT VERSION

For a 60,000 psi calibration system, use a manufacturer-matched high-pressure cone-and-thread system such as Parker Autoclave Engineers F-style high-pressure connections, with compatible tubing, glands, collars, valves, adapters, and test ports. Do not assume an NPT, BSP, or ordinary quick-connect adapter can carry 60k simply because its other end is labeled “HP.”

At 60k, the connection system is part of the pressure design

Parker Autoclave Engineers rates its high-pressure cone-and-thread fitting family to 60,000 psi (4,140 bar) in applicable configurations. Common connection designations include 1/4 HP (F250C), 3/8 HP (F375C), and 9/16 HP (F562C).

These are not pipe threads. The metal cone forms the pressure seal, while the gland and collar retain the tubing or adapter. Parker specifies matching high-pressure tubing and calls for weep-hole provisions in cone-and-thread ports.

Correct baseline

HP cone-and-thread throughout

Use the same 60k-rated connection family for source, tubing, valves, adapters, manifolds, reference, and DUT wherever possible.

Wrong assumption

“HP to NPT” does not mean 60k NPT

An adapter may have a 1/4 HP end but be limited by the converted NPT/BSP/metric end to a much lower pressure.

What does a 60k fitting stack look like?

A typical ultra-high-pressure calibration path is intentionally simple:

  1. 60,000 psi hydraulic pressure source
  2. 60k-rated HP cone-and-thread outlet
  3. matching high-pressure tubing or hose assembly
  4. 60k-rated isolation valve / manifold if required
  5. HP-to-HP adapter only where necessary
  6. 60k reference and DUT with compatible high-pressure ports

Additel's 960 pump is an example of a calibration source designed to generate up to 60,000 psi. Its pressure path must still be connected to equally rated downstream hardware.

Can I convert 60k to NPT?

Not and still assume the connection is rated to 60,000 psi. This is one of the most important distinctions in high-pressure adapter catalogs.

In Additel's ADT104-HP family, the 1/4 HP male to 1/4 HP male adapter is listed to 60,000 psi, while common 1/4 HP-to-NPT adapters are listed to 15,000 psi. The adapter's rating drops because the converted connection becomes the limiting interface.

A 60k source does not make every attached adapter a 60k adapter.

Once you convert from a 60k HP connection to a lower-rated NPT, BSP, metric, hose, valve, or DUT port, the whole assembly is limited to that lower rating.

What fittings should I actually buy for a 60k calibration bench?

Start from the pressure source's exact outlet standard, then stay inside that manufacturer's 60k-rated ecosystem for the pressure path. A practical parts list normally includes:

  • Matching HP glands and collars for each tubing size.
  • Manufacturer-approved high-pressure tubing prepared to the specified cone-and-thread geometry.
  • HP couplings or straight adapters for joining components without changing connection family.
  • 60k-rated valves where isolation or vent control is needed.
  • 60k-rated tees/manifolds only if the test layout requires multiple ports.
  • HP-to-HP adapters for the reference and DUT when they use the same compatible family.
  • Correct coning/threading tools or factory-prepared tubing if building rigid tubing runs.

What I would not put in a 60k pressure path

  • Ordinary NPT or BSP plumbing fittings.
  • Adapters whose datasheet stops at 10k, 15k, 20k, or 40k.
  • Standard compression fittings unless the exact system is explicitly rated to 60k.
  • General-purpose flexible hoses or quick disconnects.
  • Unmarked tubing with unknown wall thickness, material, or heat treatment.
  • A long adapter stack combining multiple connection families.

Why metal-to-metal cone-and-thread is used

At ultra-high pressure, the connection must resist large separating forces and repeated pressure cycles while maintaining a predictable sealing geometry. Cone-and-thread systems use a prepared metal cone for the seal and mechanically retain the tubing with a collar and gland. This avoids relying on tapered pipe-thread interference or a soft sealing element as the primary 60k seal.

60k also changes inspection and assembly requirements

The pressure rating alone is not enough. Follow the manufacturer's tubing preparation dimensions, gland torque, lubrication instructions, weep-hole requirements, material compatibility, and inspection criteria. Damage to a cone, threads, gland, collar, or tubing end can make an otherwise correct component unsafe to reuse.

Best practical rule:

At 60,000 psi, minimize adapters. Standardize the bench around one high-pressure connection family and only change connection type after the pressure has been reduced to a level supported by the converted fitting.
