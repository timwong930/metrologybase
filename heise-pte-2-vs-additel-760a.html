Heise PTE-2 vs. Additel 760A: similar jobs, very different pressure workflows

The Heise PTE-2 and Additel ADT760A can both serve serious field calibration work, but they are not true one-for-one competitors. The PTE-2 prioritizes modular measurement range and hazardous-area flexibility; the 760A prioritizes automated pressure generation and an all-in-one transmitter workflow.

Manufacturer-sourced comparison · Pressure, electrical, logging and workflow

Choose the PTE-2 when

Range and modular measurement matter most.

Its interchangeable pressure modules cover 0.25 inH₂O through 10,000 psi, it can display multiple parameters, and optional intrinsically safe versions target hazardous locations.

Choose the 760A when

You want the pressure source inside the calibrator.

The 760A automatically generates and controls pressure up to 375 psi, then combines that with electrical measurement/source functions and optional HART/documenting capability.

The biggest difference: who creates the pressure?

The ADT760A contains its own electric pump and pressure controller. Enter a setpoint and the instrument generates and controls the pressure within the selected LLP, D or MA platform. That changes field work because the technician does not need to manipulate a separate hand pump for routine points up to 375 psig.

The PTE-2 is primarily a modular measurement and calibration platform. Heise specifies pressure, temperature, current and voltage measurement, dual interchangeable sensor bays and logging, but does not list an internal pressure-generation pump. In practice it is paired with an external source—such as a pneumatic or hydraulic hand pump—when you need to apply pressure to the device under test.

Do not compare only the pressure-range numbers. PTE-2's 10,000 psi figure is a measurement-module range. The 760A's 375 psi figure is the maximum integrated automatic generation/control envelope on the MA platform. They describe different capabilities.

Where the PTE-2 is stronger

1. Much broader direct pressure measurement range

The PTE-2's interchangeable pressure modules run from very low differential ranges to 10,000 psi. That makes it easier to build one modular measurement platform around low pressure, general process pressure and higher-pressure reference work. The 760A can use external pressure modules for measurement, but its defining advantage is not a 10,000 psi internal source—it is automatic pressure generation within its own lower-pressure envelope.

2. Hazardous-area configurations

Heise explicitly offers optional FM, CSA and ATEX intrinsically safe PTE-2 versions. For technicians working in refineries, chemical plants, pipeline applications or other classified locations, that can be a decisive requirement rather than a secondary feature.

3. Dual sensor-bay flexibility

The PTE-2 has two interchangeable pressure/temperature module bays and can display up to three measurements simultaneously. That is useful when the workflow centers on comparing pressure references, combining pressure and temperature, or moving one handheld base across many sensor ranges.

Where the 760A is stronger

1. Automatic pressure generation saves technician time

The 760A's internal pump/controller is the feature that most changes the day-to-day workflow. For a five-point transmitter or gauge calibration, the instrument can drive setpoints automatically instead of requiring the technician to squeeze, vent and fine-adjust a separate pump at every point.

2. Better best-case internal pressure accuracy

Selected ADT155 modules in the D and MA versions are specified to 0.01% FS, compared with the PTE-2's best published module accuracy of ±0.025% of span. That does not mean every 760A configuration is more accurate: low-pressure LLP configurations have different specifications, and module range selection still determines absolute error.

3. Stronger documenting and HART workflow

The -DL 760A variants add a full HART field communicator, up to 1,000 documented tasks and up to 1,000,000 logged readings. For transmitter-heavy process plants, that makes the 760A closer to a one-box field workflow rather than simply a pressure reference.

Bottom line

The PTE-2 is the better modular measurement standard; the 760A is the better automatic pressure-calibration workflow. If your work is mostly under 375 psi and technician time matters, the 760A has the stronger integrated design. If you need pressure measurement through 10,000 psi, interchangeable pressure/temperature modules, or an intrinsically safe handheld platform, the PTE-2 has advantages the 760A does not directly replace.
