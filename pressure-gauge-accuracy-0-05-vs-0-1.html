0.05% pressure gauge vs. 0.1%: what is the actual difference?

A 0.05% gauge is not automatically “twice as good” in every real-world situation. The accuracy basis — full scale, span, or reading — matters just as much as the percentage. Here is how to compare the specifications, calculate the real error, and choose a gauge that actually fits the job.

SHORT VERSION

If two gauges use the same range and the same accuracy basis, a 0.05% gauge permits half the error of a 0.1% gauge. But a 0.1% of-reading gauge can outperform a 0.05% full-scale gauge through much of the lower half of its range. Always compare the complete specification, not just the percentage printed in the brochure.

Start with the simplest comparison

Assume two 0–1,000 psi gauges are both specified as a percentage of full scale.

0.05% of full scale
±0.5 psi

Maximum stated error anywhere across a 0–1,000 psi range.

0.1% of full scale
±1.0 psi

Twice the maximum stated error on the same range.

Difference
2×

When the basis and pressure range are identical, 0.05% is twice as accurate numerically.

Full-scale error = pressure range × accuracy %

For a 1,000 psi gauge, 0.05% FS is 1,000 × 0.0005 = 0.5 psi. A 0.1% FS gauge is 1,000 × 0.001 = 1.0 psi. That error limit stays the same at 100 psi, 500 psi, and 1,000 psi because the specification is tied to the range, not the reading.

Where people get tripped up: % full scale vs. % of reading

A percentage of reading behaves differently. Instead of calculating the error from the entire gauge range, you calculate it from the pressure being measured.

Of-reading error = current reading × accuracy %

*Pure mathematical comparison only. Real gauges often add a low-end floor or switch to an FS specification below a stated percentage of range. Use the manufacturer's full accuracy table.

The crossover is 50% of range.

For the simplified example above, 0.1% of reading and 0.05% of full scale are equal at 50% of range. Above 50%, 0.05% FS is tighter. Below 50%, 0.1% of reading is tighter. This is why “0.05 vs. 0.1” is incomplete without the basis.

When is 0.05% worth paying for?

0.05% is usually worth it when the gauge is functioning as a reference standard, when you are calibrating tighter-tolerance devices, or when your procedure needs more test uncertainty ratio headroom. It can also reduce the number of ranges needed when your workload spans several DUT classes.

If you are simply verifying a process gauge with a 1% or 1.6% accuracy class, a properly ranged 0.1% reference may already be more than adequate. Buying more accuracy than the uncertainty budget or procedure can use does not automatically improve the calibration.

Accuracy is not the same thing as measurement uncertainty.

A 0.05% accuracy specification can be one input to an uncertainty budget, but it is not automatically the expanded uncertainty of your calibration. Reference calibration uncertainty, resolution, repeatability, pressure generation, temperature, head height, media, hysteresis, and the DUT all matter.

What does this mean for a 4:1 TUR?

As a quick screening check only, a 0.05% FS reference aligns numerically with a 0.2% FS DUT at a 4:1 ratio, while a 0.1% FS reference aligns with a 0.4% FS DUT. Real calibration decisions should use the uncertainty required by your procedure or quality system rather than substituting the catalog accuracy number for a complete uncertainty analysis.

Three things to check before ordering

1. Accuracy basis. Confirm whether the percentage is of full scale, span, reading, or a combination with a floor. 2. Exact pressure range. A tighter percentage on an oversized range can still produce more absolute error than a looser percentage on a correctly sized range. 3. The complete accuracy statement. Look for temperature compensation, stability period, vacuum behavior, low-end floors, resolution, and whether calibration data is included.
