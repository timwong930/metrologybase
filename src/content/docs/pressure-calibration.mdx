# Pressure calibration

Pressure work often starts with a simple question: what type of pressure are you actually measuring?

## Common pressure types

- **Gauge pressure** is measured relative to local atmospheric pressure.
- **Absolute pressure** is measured relative to a perfect vacuum.
- **Differential pressure** is the difference between two pressure points.

Each one uses the same basic physics but answers a different question.

## What matters in calibration

- Range and resolution of the reference
- Stability of the source
- Leakage and setup quality
- Units and conversion mistakes
- Environmental temperature

## TUR in plain language

**TUR** usually means test uncertainty ratio. It compares the tolerance or spec you care about to the uncertainty of the test process.

A higher ratio generally gives more confidence that the test can distinguish pass from fail, but the number alone is not the whole story.

## Practical takeaway

For pressure work, always confirm:

- the pressure type,
- the expected range,
- the unit,
- and whether the setup is stable enough for the decision you want to make.
