# Metrology basics

Metrology is the science of measurement. In practice, that means understanding how a reading is made, how trustworthy it is, and how much uncertainty follows it around.

## Calibration, verification, and adjustment

- **Calibration** compares an instrument or standard against a reference and records the relationship.
- **Verification** checks whether something meets a requirement or passes a tolerance.
- **Adjustment** changes the device so its output better matches a reference.

A device can be calibrated without being adjusted. It can also be verified after calibration to confirm it still meets a target.

## Accuracy and precision

- **Accuracy** is how close a result is to the true or accepted value.
- **Precision** is how closely repeated results agree with each other.

A system can be precise but not accurate if it repeats the same wrong result.

## Uncertainty

Uncertainty is the doubt attached to a result. It does not mean the result is bad; it means the measurement has a bounded level of confidence and needs context.

### Practical habit

When reading a report or spec, ask:

1. What was measured?
2. What reference was used?
3. What uncertainty or tolerance applies?
4. What environmental or procedural factors could move the result?
