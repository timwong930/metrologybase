# TUR Basics

TUR means **test uncertainty ratio**. It compares the uncertainty of the reference system to the tolerance of the item being tested.

## Plain-English explanation

A higher TUR usually means the reference is small enough compared with the tolerance to make the test more comfortable. A lower TUR means the reference uncertainty takes up more of the tolerance band.

TUR is helpful, but it is not magic. It does not replace good procedures, stable equipment, or sensible range selection.

## Practical example

If a DUT has a tolerance of **±1.0 psi** and your reference contributes **±0.25 psi** uncertainty, the TUR is roughly **4:1**.

That may be acceptable in one procedure and not enough in another, depending on the required decision rule.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not treat TUR as a universal pass/fail rule. The right ratio depends on the measurement goal, the specification, and the risk you are willing to accept.

## Short summary

TUR is a quick way to compare uncertainty against tolerance. It is useful, but it should be read together with the procedure and the acceptance criteria.
