# Pressure Calibration

Pressure calibration sounds complicated at first, but the core ideas are simple: know what kind of pressure you are measuring, know the unit, and know how accurate your setup really is.

Use this section as a quick map before you go deeper into the individual topics.

## What you will find here

- **Gauge, absolute, and differential pressure** — what the reference point means
- **Pressure units** — how to move between psi, bar, Pa, and related units
- **Full-scale accuracy** — how to read common spec statements
- **TUR basics** — a simple way to think about reference quality
- **Choosing a pressure range** — how to avoid an awkward or oversized setup

## Practical example

If a procedure asks for **100 psig**, you need to work in gauge pressure, not absolute pressure. If the same procedure also gives a tolerance of **±0.5% FS**, you need to understand what full scale means before you decide whether the setup is good enough.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not assume all pressure numbers mean the same thing. A value written as psig, psia, or psid can point to different reference points even when the numeric value looks identical.

## Short summary

Pressure calibration is mostly about definitions, units, and uncertainty. If those three pieces are clear, the rest becomes much easier.
