# Gauge vs Absolute vs Differential Pressure

The unit may stay the same, but the reference point changes the meaning of the number.

- **Gauge pressure** is measured relative to local atmospheric pressure.
- **Absolute pressure** is measured relative to a perfect vacuum.
- **Differential pressure** is the difference between two pressure points.

## Plain-English explanation

Think of gauge pressure as "pressure above the air around you." Absolute pressure counts everything from zero vacuum. Differential pressure cares only about the difference between two ports, no matter what the surrounding atmosphere is doing.

## Practical example

A reading of **100 psig** means the pressure is 100 psi above atmosphere. The same system might be around **114.7 psia** at sea level because atmospheric pressure is added on top of the gauge reading.

If a filter monitor shows **2.0 inH2O differential**, it is comparing upstream and downstream pressure, not comparing either port to atmosphere.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not convert gauge pressure to absolute pressure by changing the unit alone. You also need the local atmospheric pressure if the value depends on the reference point.

## Short summary

Always check the reference point first. Gauge, absolute, and differential pressure are related, but they are not interchangeable.
