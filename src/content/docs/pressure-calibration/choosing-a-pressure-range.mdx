# Choosing a Pressure Range

The best pressure range is not always the biggest one. You want a range that covers the DUT, fits your target uncertainty, and leaves enough room for useful test points.

## Plain-English explanation

If the range is too small, you may overload the reference or run out of span. If the range is too large, the useful part of the scale may be too coarse for the job.

A good range choice usually balances three things:

1. The DUT range
2. The uncertainty target
3. The test points you need to cover

## Practical example

If you calibrate a **0 to 300 psi** gauge, a **0 to 600 psi** standard may be a reasonable fit when you need overhead for test points and future jobs.

But if the same reference is too coarse at low pressure, a smaller range or a second standard may produce a better result.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not buy or choose the highest range just because it seems versatile. A huge range can make low-pressure work awkward and may reduce your ability to meet uncertainty targets.

## Short summary

Pick the range for the work you actually do, not for the largest number you might someday see.
