# Pressure Units

Pressure units are just different ways of writing the same physical idea. The hard part is not the math; it is making sure you are converting the right kind of pressure and the right unit definition.

## Plain-English explanation

Metrology work often uses a mix of **psi**, **bar**, **kPa**, **Pa**, **MPa**, **inH2O**, **inHg**, **mmHg**, and **mbar**. Some are metric, some are imperial, and some show up because a specific industry prefers them.

A converter can help, but it cannot tell you whether the original value was gauge, absolute, or differential.

## Practical example

If a procedure says **250 mbar**, that is the same as **25 kPa** or about **3.63 psi**.

That helps you compare ranges, but you still need to know whether the 250 mbar value is gauge or absolute pressure.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not use a unit conversion to solve a reference-point problem. **100 kPa gauge** and **100 kPa absolute** are not the same physical condition.

## Short summary

Unit conversion changes the scale, not the meaning. Always confirm both the unit and the pressure type.
