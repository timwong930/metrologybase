# Full-Scale Accuracy

Full-scale accuracy is a common way to state how far a device may be from the true value, but it is easy to misread.

## Plain-English explanation

When a spec says **±0.1% FS**, the error limit is based on the device's full range, not on the actual reading.

That means a 1000 psi instrument with ±0.1% FS accuracy has an error limit of about ±1 psi across the range, whether you are reading 50 psi or 900 psi.

## Practical example

A 0 to 1000 psi standard with ±0.1% FS accuracy has a full-scale error band of ±1 psi.

If you are calibrating a 0 to 100 psi gauge, that may be fine or it may be too loose depending on the procedure and the uncertainty target.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not assume **0.1% FS** means **0.1% of the reading**. That is a different spec basis and can lead to the wrong acceptance decision.

## Short summary

Always ask: percent of what? Full scale, reading, span, or something else. The answer changes the size of the error band.
