How to use the Additel ADT875 (non-PC)

The standard ADT875 is a precision temperature source, not a built-in RTD, thermocouple or HART meter. Here's the actual touchscreen procedure for the ADT875-155, -350 and -660, plus how to collect defensible calibration results using an external readout.

Scope: standard ADT875, without “PC” in the model number.

Do not follow ADT875PC instructions for the DUT electrical channels, 24 V loop power, reference-PRT connector, HART communication or documenting tasks. Those capabilities require the PC electronics. The non-PC version still controls the dry-well block and displays its internal temperature.

Required dry-out after storage.

The ADT875 manual requires the approximately 2-hour “Dry-out”/dehumidification cycle before initial use, after humid storage, or after the instrument has been idle for more than 10 days. Complete that cycle before ordinary calibration; see the manual's Safety Information (printed p. 2) and §5.3 (printed p. 107). Do not improvise a substitute heating routine.

Step by step: operate the non-PC ADT875

Choose the insert and load the probes safely

With the block cool, use the proper insert-removal tool and place the model-matched insert into the well. Select bores that fit the DUT and, for a comparison calibration, a separate reference probe. Insert to an adequate working depth; confirm neither probe bottoms out or is mechanically stressed. Use the silica-gel and insulation plugs where directed for the 155, or the thermal shield for the 350/660.

Power on; set the temperature units

Power on the ADT875. Tap the top-right system menu → Setup → Personalization → Temperature Unit and choose °C, °F or K. You can also tap the temperature unit shown in the temperature window to change it. This is the navigation in manual §2.2 (printed p. 19).

Touchscreen menu path (manual §2.2)

Top-right system menu→Setup→Personalization→Temperature Unit→°C / °F / K

Schematic navigation, not a photograph of the screen. You may also tap the unit text in the main temperature panel.

Check the control settings

Tap the control/settings icon at the left of the temperature-output window. In §2.3, the manual lists Stability Tolerance, Stabilization Time (1–120 min), Set Point Tolerance, Control Rate and optional Set Point Limits. Define appropriate limits and settling criteria for your procedure. On a non-PC unit, use INT (internal control sensor), not the PC-only EXT reference sensor.

Control-program parameters (manual §2.3)

  • Stability tolerance: allowable block temperature variation.
  • Stabilization time: 1–120 minutes (programmed time criterion).
  • Setpoint tolerance: maximum difference of actual block reading vs target.
  • Control rate: fastest or a customized temperature-change rate.
  • Setpoint limits: restrict allowed output range.
  • Reference: INT on standard non-PC; external PRT control is a PC option.

Enter a setpoint

On the main temperature-output panel, tap the INT target temperature or the real-time temperature area. Enter the target with the on-screen number pad, then press Enter/check. According to manual §2.3 (printed p. 21), accepting the target starts temperature control automatically. Stay within the actual model's range and any enabled setpoint limits.

Watch stabilization; pause or resume if needed

The panel shows target and actual internal temperatures. The Start/Play and Pause controls appear to the right of the temperature window. When the programmed stability conditions are met, the temperature display turns green and a beep sounds. This is a controller stability indication—allow any additional soak time your procedure requires and confirm the separate reference is stable before recording DUT results.

Read, compare and record each test point

Read the DUT using its own indicator or a separate RTD/TC meter; for lower-uncertainty comparison work, read a calibrated reference PRT with its own compatible thermometer. Record the actual reference temperature, DUT indication, direction of approach (up/down) and time/stability criterion. Repeat at the required temperatures; include increasing and decreasing points when checking hysteresis.

Return to a safe temperature before shutdown

Command a safe setpoint and allow the block to cool. Do not touch or remove the probes/insert while the over-50°C high-temperature warning is active (manual Safety Information). Once cool and safe, stop temperature control, switch off, then unplug. Do not move a hot unit or pour liquid into the dry well.

How to get real calibration data without the PC option

The key difference is that the standard ADT875 supplies a controlled temperature, but your measurement and records are external. This works for RTDs, thermocouples, dial thermometers, temperature switches and transmitters when paired with the appropriate separate measuring equipment.

Worked example: temperature error at 100°C

Setpoint: 100.00°C · reference thermometer: 100.08°C · DUT: 100.30°C. The DUT's indication error at the measured reference temperature is +0.22°C (100.30 − 100.08). Do not call this a pass/fail result until you apply the DUT tolerance and your uncertainty/decision rule. The ADT875's displayed setpoint is not a substitute for a measured independent reference.

Transmitter example: to calibrate a 0–200°C, 4–20 mA transmitter with a standard ADT875, use a separately powered loop and external mA meter or process calibrator; generate the temperature with the dry well and compare the output to the expected current based on the measured reference temperature. You cannot connect the loop directly to non-PC ADT875 electrical terminals because those inputs/loop power are not fitted.

Automatic stepping: the manual describes a Step Test application (§5.5, printed pp. 109–110), including setpoints, dwell times and repeat cycles. Where the menu is available on the installed firmware, it can assist with source temperature sequencing, but it does not add missing non-PC input terminals or PC task documentation. Verify the installed software and record reference/DUT values externally.

What about short probes or short-stem transmitters?

Not every sensor has enough insertion length for a conventional dry-block comparison. A short probe may experience substantial heat loss along its stem, leaving its sensing element at a different temperature than the displayed block reading. Simply choosing a tighter hole is not always enough.

Short-probe setup decisions

  • Check the sensing-element location, not just total probe length.
  • Confirm a compatible short-probe kit is specified for your ADT875-155.
  • Consider a suitably designed portable liquid bath if immersion and sensor geometry cannot be met.
  • Determine correction and uncertainty using a traceable reference rather than assuming internal-display accuracy.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using a PC screenshot to wire a non-PC unit

Do not treat front-panel HART, mA or external reference terminals in manufacturer PC photos as present on the base instrument. Use an independent instrument.

Recording target instead of reference temperature

The source setpoint is what you asked the block to do. Actual calibration readings come from an appropriate reference, with its own uncertainty.

Ignoring sensor immersion

Confirm diameter, probe seating, proximity of the sensing elements, well depth and enough settling time for both DUT and reference.

Removing a hot insert

Never handle the insert or probe while the over-50°C safety warning is active. Do not place the hot instrument into its transport case.

Common operating questions

Can the non-PC ADT875 calibrate RTDs or thermocouples?

Yes—as the controlled thermal source. Connect the DUT to a separate compatible RTD/TC readout, not to PC-only ports. Use a calibrated external reference when the required uncertainty is tighter than the dry-well display accuracy allows.

Can I connect an external reference PRT to the standard ADT875?

Not to the unit's PC-only reference-measurement connector. For the standard unit, read the PRT with a separate compatible thermometer; the factory's smart PRT interface, EXT control and integrated PRT readout are PC-option functionality.

Why does the display turn green before my probe stops changing?

The green indication confirms the block met the programmed internal-controller stability conditions, not necessarily that the DUT's sensing element or external reference has equilibrated. Extend soak time and check the independently measured probe value.

What should I use for a short-stem probe?

Immersion can dominate uncertainty. Additel lists the ADT110-875-L-SHRT-IKIT for the 875-155. Confirm probe geometry and whether a liquid bath is more suitable rather than assuming an ordinary insert gives adequate depth.
