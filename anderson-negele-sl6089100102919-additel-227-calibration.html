Field calibration guide

Can you calibrate an Anderson-Negele SL with an Additel 227?

Yes. The SL is a sanitary hydrostatic level transmitter—but behind the tank-level display is a conventional pressure-to-4–20 mA relationship. Here's how to test the actual measurement chain.

Worked example: SL6089100102919 • Reference span: 291.9 inH₂O • Electrical output: 4–20 mA

Start with the sensor—not the calibrator

The Anderson-Negele SL series measures the pressure caused by the weight of liquid above its sanitary diaphragm. As the tank fills, pressure rises and the transmitter converts that pressure into a current signal interpreted as tank level.

For SL models whose calibration code follows Anderson-Negele's documented five-digit convention, the ending 02919 represents 291.9 inches of water column at the 20 mA endpoint. Confirm the nameplate and factory calibration documentation before assuming that span applies to a field-adjusted transmitter.

The exact connection and HART option of SL6089100102919 should be confirmed against its nameplate or factory order record. The SL family includes sanitary flush-diaphragm process connections; don't assume an ordinary NPT pressure port.

Sources: Anderson-Negele SL product overview · SL technical manual.

What equipment connects to what?

The Additel 227 provides loop supply and current measurement and reads the separate reference pressure module. It does not produce pressure.

Critical mechanical detail: Use the manufacturer's suitable hygienic flush-mount adapter/fixture so the sensitive diaphragm is exposed to controlled test pressure without damage. Do not apply pressure directly to an unprotected diaphragm. Isolate, drain, clean and make the equipment safe before removing from service.

Why an ADT161 pressure module?

The 227's two pressure ports accept compatible external reference modules. For a roughly 10.54 psi span, a correctly calibrated 15 psi gauge reference module may be appropriate after verifying required uncertainty, pressure mode and fittings. Additel's ADT912A pump is a potential low-pressure source.

Choose the module accuracy and uncertainty budget based on the SL's acceptance limits. The external pressure module—not the 227's electrical accuracy alone—determines the pressure-reference performance.

A repeatable five-point test

1. Prepare

Review the SL nameplate, range, tolerance and medium compatibility. Record the as-found configuration. Install the sensor with a suitable sanitary fixture.

2. Connect

Connect a pump and reference module to the pressure fixture; wire the SL to the ADT227's 24 V loop supply and mA measurement per the manuals.

3. Zero

Vent safely and verify the reading near 4.000 mA. Do not trim the transmitter until the original as-found readings have been captured.

4. Step upward

Apply 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% pressure, allowing readings to stabilize. Record actual pressure, expected current and measured current.

5. Return to zero

Repeat the sequence descending to check hysteresis. Use a documented acceptance criterion based on the DUT specification.

6. Document

Use the ADT227 transmitter-calibration task to record as-found/as-left results, then export or retain the calibration record.

What about HART?

The SL product family includes optional HART electronics, but the exact model's option cannot be established from the five-digit span alone. For confirmed compatible HART transmitters, the ADT227-HART adds the full HART communication application and DD library. The standard ADT227 can still carry out the analog pressure-to-current verification without assuming HART support.

Don't confuse simulation with sensor calibration: Injecting a 4–20 mA signal into the control system tests the input/display path. Applying calibrated pressure to the SL and measuring its actual mA output tests the transmitter's physical sensing chain.
